title: Silkscreen
---

Now before we get all excited, let's polish up our PCB a little bit! We're
going to do this by modifying what's called the silkscreen, this is all the
white stuff you see on the PCB, it's purely decoration and used to show
where components should go when soldering, and also to label stuff, and you
can make art with it.

<Step title="Tidy the reference designators">
- But before we get to make the art, let's move all the labels on our PCB
  to look nice and organized! Now the capacitors and stuff aren't too
  useful to us, so we can delete those silkscreen labels like "C#" or
  "R#", and just leave the important ones for the connect, the pi, etc.
- I'd suggest hiding some layers to make it easier to see, so delete all
  the stuff we don't want, and organize the stuff we do want (You can also
  hide the fab layer for now, but there's no need to delete that stuff,
  it won't be on your PCB):

  ![Layers thinned out](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaiPereira/build-a-devboard/master/journal/Pasted%20image%2020250929211638.png)

- This is how mine looks when done:

  ![Cleaned silkscreen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaiPereira/build-a-devboard/master/journal/Pasted%20image%2020250929211919.png)
</Step>

<Step title="Add your art">
- Now, let's add our own art! Try out the KiCad image converter, and also
  add some text and stuff to your PCB. I personally like to add things
  like my website, github handle, favorite animals, anime, whatever you
  want really.
- I personally use excalidraw to draw my art but you can do whatever:

  ![Art in progress](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaiPereira/build-a-devboard/master/journal/Pasted%20image%2020250929215517.png)

  ![More art](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaiPereira/build-a-devboard/master/journal/Pasted%20image%2020250929215558.png)

  ![Silkscreen final](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KaiPereira/build-a-devboard/master/journal/Pasted%20image%2020250929215613.png)
</Step>

<Checkpoint>
**And that's our PCB finished!** Run DRC one last time to make sure you
have *no errors*, and then continue on to the final step, which is getting
this thing manufactured!
</Checkpoint>
