---
title: PCB layout
---

<Warning title="100 × 100 mm, max">
Your finished board must fit within 100 × 100 mm - lay everything out
against that boundary from the start.
</Warning>

Opening the PCB editor, you should see something like this:

![PCB editor](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019dae27-a243-7319-835c-ff4e05737e34/screenshot_2026-04-20_225259.png)

Any time you change the schematic, press **F8** (or click **Update PCB
from Schematic**) to pull the changes into the layout. The two circles you
see are M3 mounting holes - press **A** to add your own.

<Step title="Define the board outline">
- The white outline is the **Edge Cuts** layer - the physical boundary
  where the PCB gets cut. Draw it by selecting `Edge.Cuts` on the right
  and using the drawing tools.
- Want something more custom than rectangles? This outline was drawn in
  Figma, converted with an image-to-DXF converter, imported into KiCad,
  then scaled against a 100 × 100 mm reference box using the measuring
  tool:

  ![cutout](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d6fcf-c1dc-7c1b-be3c-a8aac74ded7a/image.png)

- KiCad's own drawing tools work well too - a cool, unique border is
  worth the detour though. Try it!

  ![](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d6fd1-62ad-753b-81ce-07980f6496e3/image.png)
</Step>

<Step title="Place the components">
- Place every component inside the outline. The thin straight blue lines
  are **ratlines** (ratsnest lines) - unrouted connections showing which
  pads must eventually be joined by copper.
- Arrange components so the ratlines are short and don't cross - that's
  the whole game, and it makes routing dramatically easier.
- **M** moves, **R** rotates, **F** flips a component to the other side
  of the board, and **Ctrl+S** saves - do that often.
- Here's the result:

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/hww8o3.png)
</Step>
