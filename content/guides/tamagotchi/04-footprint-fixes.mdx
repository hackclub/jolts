<Warning title="Verify footprints before you route">
Even when a component looks correct in the schematic, the footprint may
carry wrong pad numbering that makes your circuit fail silently. The red
flag: any pin without a net almost always means an import went wrong.
</Warning>

## The button footprint mismatch

After importing the button from EasyEDA, the footprint looks fine at
first glance:

![KiCad imported switch footprint](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64e0-1e84-77a2-b640-9ae44ec2f111/image.png)

EasyEDA uses a **redundant 4-pin symbol**, showing all four pins even
though two pairs are internally shorted:

![EasyEDA button symbol](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64e1-af44-78ba-9264-cd050bf0913b/image.png)

KiCad uses a **cleaner 2-pin representation**:

![KiCad button symbol](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64e4-065a-7b78-947d-76c2c4166b31/image.png)

When imported, EasyEDA's pins 1 and 2 land on opposite footprint pads - but on the physical switch they're on the **same side**, internally
shorted. Pads that should be separate end up sharing a net, and the
button does nothing because one side is permanently shorted to itself.

| EasyEDA pins | Physical side    | KiCad net |
| :----------: | :--------------: | :-------: |
| 1, 2         | Side A (shorted) | Net 1     |
| 3, 4         | Side B (shorted) | Net 2     |

The switch connects Side A to Side B when pressed - the footprint must
reflect that. Confirm with the
[datasheet](https://www.lcsc.com/datasheet/C2888493.pdf) from the
[product page](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C2888493.html):

![Physical pin diagram](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64f2-0ab2-7a97-a6a2-c0e4637bd711/image.png)
![Internal schematic](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64f2-5c5b-7c82-8d01-eba0fbff8d62/image.png)

<Step title="Fix the button pad numbering">
- Double-click the footprint in the PCB editor and choose **Edit Library
  Footprint**.

  ![Edit Library Footprint dialog](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64f5-e570-70fe-a8fb-22ab0854085b/image.png)

- Renumber the pads: both pads on one physical side become **1**, both on
  the other side become **2**.

  ![Corrected pad numbering](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64f7-c4ab-78f2-adc6-450d63ee774e/image.png)

- **Ctrl+S** to save, then **Tools → Update Footprint from Library** and
  click **Update**.

  ![Update Footprint from Library menu](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d64f9-6a84-7663-a0b4-69b215dbb021/image.png)

- Not working? Delete the buttons and update from schematic again.

  ![](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d6529-b11d-7d96-830f-c7431f1d8092/image.png)
</Step>

<Step title="Fix the XIAO footprint">
- Same idea for the XIAO:

  ![Xiao](https://files.catbox.moe/5e2aze.png)

- Double-click each BAT pad, set it to **through-hole (THT)**, and give
  it a drill diameter of **0.889mm** - that's what lets you solder the
  XIAO on by melting solder into the pads.

  ![Fixing Footprint](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019dae19-2ecf-7620-a4d3-4fd2b2442dec/recording_2026-04-20_232848.gif)

- The pin numbers also disagree with the official Seeed diagram - fix
  them to match. The footprint is mirrored, so temporarily move it to the
  back layer to orient it the same way as the diagram:

  ![Footprints](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/XIAO_WiFi/back-label-6.png)

- Useful diagrams:

  ![](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019e9092-784f-741c-8e92-4dabde84ffb6/image.png)

  ![](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019e9092-573a-7d08-a428-c32b4c5da3f0/image.png)
</Step>

<Checkpoint>
Every pad has a net, the button pads read 1/1 and 2/2 by physical side,
and the XIAO's BAT pads are through-hole. Save and update the PCB from
schematic:
