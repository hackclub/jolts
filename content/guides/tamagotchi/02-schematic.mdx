title: Schematic
---

The schematic is the circuit as an idea: which pin connects to what, and
why. Everything you draw here becomes the PCB in the next page.

<Step title="Import the XIAO">
- Download the XIAO-ESP32-C3 symbol from the
  [SeeedStudio Wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/XIAO_ESP32C3_Getting_Started/) - there's also an [excellent video tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lnCvyGIQong)
  on using the board.

  ![](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d848c-2e6a-7afd-ab8b-fc6f28d32ac7/image.png)

- Press **A** (Add Symbol), search for the part, and place it:

  ![Xiao](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019d985d-8af2-746a-901e-c897ece4b3fa/image.png)

- Most symbols won't label every pin with its function (SDA, SCL, MOSI…) - they just show pin numbers. When that happens, google the MCU name +
  "pinout" and cross-reference which number maps to which function.
</Step>

<Step title="Wire up power">
- Connect GND to GND, 5V to +5V, +3V3 to 3V3, and BAT to +BATT:

  ![Power connections](https://files.catbox.moe/kgmyi6.png)

- Ground is the **reference point** for the entire circuit - the zero
  level everything else is measured against. Current flows from power,
  through components, back to ground; without it there's no return path
  and nothing works. If volts and rails are still fuzzy, read
  <ConceptLink slug="voltage" /> before continuing.
- The XIAO exposes two rails: **5V** comes from USB-C (on battery it gets
  nothing - a LiPo's ~3.7V can't supply it), while **3V3** is a stable
  3.3V generated on-board, available on USB or battery. The OLED, sensors,
  and logic pins are rated for 3.3V, so they connect to 3V3 - too much
  voltage means too much current, which damages components.
</Step>

**Why not connect the top pads?**

![](https://files.catbox.moe/ozimcj.png)

Those are JTAG debug pads - a special cable connects them to your computer
so you can pause your program mid-run and inspect the chip line by line.
USB-C handles everything this project needs, and wiring them would
complicate the PCB for little benefit. Use them if you hit a bug Serial
printing can't catch, or if you run out of GPIO pins.

<Step title="Add the OLED display">
- Import the [0.96" OLED](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C5248080.html)
  from EasyEDA into KiCad - follow the
  [easyeda2kicad guide](https://hwdocs.hackclub.dev/user-contrib-guides/easyeda2kicad/).
- Connect GND to GND, SDA to SDA, and SCL to SCL with **W**. Those two
  data lines are <ConceptLink slug="i2c" /> - one carries data, one
  carries the clock.
- I2C normally needs <ConceptLink slug="pull-up-resistors" /> on both
  lines, but the XIAO includes internal pull-ups, so no external ones here.
- The [datasheet](https://www.lcsc.com/datasheet/C5248080.pdf) on the LCSC
  page says VCC wants 3V3, so:

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/34nu2g.png)
</Step>

<Step title="Add the buttons">
- A Tamagotchi has three buttons. Wire each one **active-low**: one side
  to **GND**, the other to a **GPIO pin** with the XIAO's internal
  pull-up enabled.

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/bclgxg.png)

- "Active-low" means pressed = LOW (0V): idle, the pull-up holds the pin
  HIGH at 3.3V; pressing connects it to ground. It's the most common
  approach because it needs nothing but the button and a ground wire.
- GPIO stands for General Purpose Input/Output - the programmable pins
  that read buttons or drive buzzers. On the XIAO, D0–D10 are GPIOs.
</Step>

<Step title="Add the buzzer">
- Add a buzzer ([this one](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C49246964.html)) - one pin to a GPIO output, the other to GND, so the GPIO can drive it
  with a square-wave tone.

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/4ksaud.png)
</Step>

<Step title="Power & battery">
- The XIAO has built-in battery charging: connect a LiPo directly to the
  **BAT** pin. The board runs wirelessly and recharges whenever USB-C is
  plugged in.
- BAT connects *only* to the battery - it's a dedicated charging pin,
  separate from the 3.3V rail. GND is shared across everything; every
  component needs the common reference.

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/wbgbys.png)

- Place no-connect flags on every unused pin: press **Q**, then click the
  pin.

  ![](https://files.catbox.moe/3dtovm.png)
</Step>

<Warning title="Schematic hygiene">
Inputs generally go on the left, outputs on the right - signal flows like
reading a book. You don't have to follow it strictly, but consistency
makes your schematic much easier to debug later. And don't forget those
no-connect flags!
</Warning>

<Step title="Assign footprints">
- The schematic is done - now every symbol needs a physical footprint.
  Click this:

  ![](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7fdab501-5c92-4df0-b032-ab40fdc7018a)

- **Battery connector:** 2.54mm male headers - the standard pin spacing
  on most hobby electronics.
- **Buzzer:** the [AliExpress listing](https://www.aliexpress.us/item/3256810135642750.html)
  says 12 × 9.5mm (12mm diameter, 9.5mm pin spacing) - that's the
  `Buzzer_12x9.5RM7.6` footprint. Careful: EasyEDA's buzzer footprint uses
  7mm spacing instead of 9.5mm.
- **Buttons:** import from [LCSC C2888493](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C2888493.html).
- **XIAO:** the `XIAO-ESP32-C3-DIP-SMD` footprint from the
  [official footprint library](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/XIAO-KiCad-Library/New_XIAO_Series_Footprints.zip) - we'll modify its SMD pads to through-hole later so the XIAO solders on
  directly.
- **OLED:** the EasyEDA import from earlier.

  ![Footprint assignment](https://files.catbox.moe/mfchm6.png)
</Step>
